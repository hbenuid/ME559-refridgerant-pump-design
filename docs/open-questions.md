# Open questions

Questions we can't answer yet, with the working assumption we use in the meantime. When one is answered, move it to
[Answered](#answered) with the date, and add an entry to [decisions.md](decisions.md) if it settles a design choice.

| Q2 | What are the smallest practical eccentricity and rotor length, and what flow do they give? | Flow is now an output of the geometry ([D10](decisions.md)). It sets motor size, power and cost. | Method in [functional-design.md](functional-design.md#sizing). Needs a leakage model. | Team to work out |
| Q3 | Will oil (POE) be present in the R-134a? If so, which lubricant, how much, and at what ratio to the refrigerant? | Oil-free means vanes and bearings run on a fluid thinner than water. Oil changes elastomer compatibility. The pump is standalone, so no compressor oil arrives unless we add it on purpose. | Kept open. Design for oil-free wear (worst case) and check materials against POE too. The team expects to add a lubricant (2026-11-05); type, amount and ratio are their open questions ([references.md](references.md#team-notes)). | Open; leaning toward oil |
| Q4 | Is 3600 RPM the actual running speed or the synchronous speed of a 2-pole motor? | An induction motor slips to about 3450–3550 RPM, which lowers flow by 1–4%. | 2-pole induction motor on 60 Hz; 3600 used as an upper bound. | Open |

That may be the other 30%. | Ask instructor |

| Q21 | Which consumables (assembly oil, thread locker, grease) can be used inside the housing? | They sit in R-134a for the life of the pump, and they go in the BOM. Assembly oil also bears on [Q3](open-questions.md). | Check each against R-134a and POE; prefer mechanical locking to thread locker inside the housing ([materials.md](materials.md#consumables)). | Research |
| Q22 | Does heat inside the housing raise any part, or the liquid entering the pump, above 150 °F? | The motor is outside since [D33](decisions.md). The heat left in the liquid comes from the pump's own losses (at most about 1.7 °F, [requirements.md](requirements.md#real-fluid-check)) and, with a metal containment can, eddy-current loss in the can ([Q28](open-questions.md)). If parts run hot, the Granta screen needs a higher maximum service temperature ([materials.md](materials.md#selection-in-granta), Stage 1), and expansion changes clearances further. Polymer vanes, elastomer seals and the coupling magnets are the most sensitive. | Every part is at fluid temperature, so 150 °F is the upper limit. | Open |
| Q27 | How is the pump protected against overpressure, and where does the relief vent? | A positive-displacement pump against a blocked outlet, or liquid trapped and warming, raises pressure within moments. Without a relief, the casing, bolts or O-rings fail, or the coupling slips. The team proposes a spring-loaded relief valve in the pump head set at 225 psig, plus a small thermal relief on any section that can trap liquid ([references.md](references.md#team-notes)). Two checks from [calcs/pressure_cases.py](../calcs/pressure_cases.py): a relief that vents at 225 psig gauge would open in an idle pump at 139 °F, inside the 150 °F range, and venting refrigerant is prohibited (EPA Section 608). A liquid-full pump with both isolation valves closed rises from 55 to about 360 psig after warming 5 °F. | A relief is needed. One option: an internal relief returning outlet flow to the inlet, set by pressure difference so the idle pressure can't open it, plus a thermal relief that vents back into the suction line. Types and set points to research. | Research |
| Q28 | Which containment can: metal (316 stainless, Hastelloy) or non-metallic (carbon-fibre PEEK, ceramic)? | The can holds the 300 psig design pressure ([D32](decisions.md)) and sits in the coupling's magnetic gap. A metal can is strong and easy to make, but eddy currents turn part of the shaft power into heat in the liquid ([Q22](open-questions.md)). A non-metallic can has no eddy loss but costs more; a polymer needs its creep and R-134a permeation checked at 300 psig, and ceramic is brittle. | Not chosen. | Open |
| Q29 | What inlet and outlet port and connection sizes? | Raised in the team notes (entrance and exit cross-sectional areas). The pressure drop into the vane cells eats into the inlet's margin to boiling in the [real-fluid check](requirements.md#real-fluid-check), and the connection size ties to [Q14](open-questions.md). | Sized once the flow is known ([Q2](open-questions.md)). | Team to work out |
